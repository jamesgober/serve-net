# serve-net v0.5.0 - Reactor

**Thousands of connections per worker.** v0.5.0 moves serve-net onto net-io 0.5's reactor driver, adds streaming bodies and an offload pool for slow work, and fixes a drain that could lose requests during a restart under load.

## What's new

- **The reactor.** `Workers::auto()` now picks net-io's reactor: one worker per logical CPU, each holding thousands of connections. `Workers::reactor(n)` picks it by name, and `Workers::blocking(n)` keeps the thread-per-connection pool. In CI, 10,000 idle keep-alive connections held at 76 to 90 MiB on Linux, macOS, and Windows, and every one answered afterwards.
- **Streaming.** `BodyPolicy::Stream` hands the service each piece of a request body as it arrives (`HttpBody::Chunk`), and `HttpReply::stream` with `Outcome::More` sends a reply in parts. The service is asked for the next part only after the last one is written, so a slow reader holds back the service, not memory.
- **`Offload`.** `Io` and `Cpu` services run on a bounded pool you provide (`OffloadPool`, `OffloadJob`), so slow work never holds a reactor worker. A full pool answers the busy refusal.
- **`InFlightCap::reserve`.** Capacity kept for one lane, out of reach of ordinary traffic.
- **`Budget::dedicated_workers`.** Seats kept for one profile, such as an admin listener, that a flood on other listeners cannot take.
- **`WatchdogPinger`.** Pings the service manager only while every worker is healthy, so a stuck worker gets the process restarted.
- **`catch_service_panics`.** A panicking service answers 500 and closes its connection instead of taking down the worker.
- **`docs/PLATFORM-NOTES.md`.** What each feature does on Linux, macOS, and Windows.

## Fixed

- **A restart could lose requests.** A drain could reset connections accepted as the door closed, with their request unread: about 4 percent of restarts under load lost requests. A drain now waits for accepting to settle first (about 3 ms). 0 of 400 restart runs lost a request after the fix.
- **Small blocking pools served one client at a time.** A pool of 17 threads or fewer with no `max_connections` had a soft connection cap of 1. The headroom is now at most a quarter of the pool.

## Known issues

- **Restarts on Windows (T-30).** The guarantee that a restart through a shared socket refuses and resets nothing is tested on Linux and macOS only. On Windows, two servers sharing a socket lost connections on CI's small runners, so the promise is not made there yet. Plan a Windows restart around a front end that retries. This stays open until it is fixed, at the latest before any 1.0 release candidate.
- **Idle memory.** An idle keep-alive connection costs about 9 KB against a 4 KiB target. The rest waits on a net-io change and is due in 0.6.0.
- **Low descriptor limits.** With no `max_connections` and a low `ulimit -n` (256 on a stock macOS), connections past the limit are reset rather than answered 503. Set `max_connections` under the limit, or raise the limit.
- **An injected clock on the reactor.** With a manual clock, a drain on the reactor does not finish. Real clocks are not affected; tests that use a manual clock run on the blocking pool.
- **`dedicated_workers` on the reactor** keeps connection seats, not threads: the profile's connections still share workers with the rest.

Each is tracked in the pre-1.0 polish register in `dev/ROADMAP.md`.

## Moved to 0.6.0

WebSocket upgrades come first in 0.6.0. FastCGI (Linux and macOS first) and `Http1OrH2ByAlpn` with an end-to-end TLS test wait for fcgi-net and tls-net. A run under real systemd moves with them.

## Upgrading from 0.4.0

The default driver is now the reactor. A service that blocks delays every connection on its worker: mark it `WorkClass::Io` or `Cpu` and use `Offload`, or choose `Workers::blocking(n)`. Keep-alive shortening under pressure now applies to the blocking driver only. `Outcome` and `BodyPolicy` have a new variant each; both are non-exhaustive. serve-net now depends on net-io 0.5 and http-net 0.9.

## What's next

`0.6.0`: WebSocket upgrades, the performance pass against the targets in `dev/PLAN.md`, the idle memory target, and FastCGI and TLS as their crates arrive.
